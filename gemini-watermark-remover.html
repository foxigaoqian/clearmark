Why the Gemini sparkle logo is a problem

Google stamps a four-point sparkle logo on every image and video its AI models generate — Gemini, Veo, Imagen, Nano Banana. When you're just playing around, it's harmless. But the moment you use AI generations for real work — client mockups, marketing assets, thumbnails, presentations — that little logo undercuts the whole thing. It flags the piece as AI-made before anyone reads a word, and it tells your client exactly which tool you used before you've shown your value.

Because the Gemini watermark always sits in a known corner position, it's actually one of the easiest marks to remove cleanly: ClearMark ships with a Gemini preset mask calibrated to the sparkle logo, so you often don't even need to draw anything. The AI inpainting then reconstructs the pixels underneath, and because everything runs locally in your browser via WebAssembly, your generations never touch a server.
